
## Time Value
- The part of an option's price that is attributed to time remaining
- Based on the amount of time the option has no intrinsic value
- Time value on an option has no intrinsic value; it's entirely based on time (like "it's out of the money")
- Its entire worth is "based off time value"

## Exercise
- This occurs when the owner of an option invokes the right entailed in the option contract
- On expiry, it means the option automatically contacts the stock (or other asset) on the other side of the option requiring the option seller to take the other side of the trade there

## Assignment
- When an option seller (or writer) is obligated to fulfil their assignment
- Most make good on their verily (or her) obligation
- Must make good on or sell the underlying stock at the strike price

## What is Volatility?
- As an individual trader, volatility is simply the amount the stock price changes — what you really only need to concern yourself with:
  - Historical volatility
  - Implied volatility

### Historical Volatility
- Historical volatility is identical to traders as HV; the annualised description of the stock's price movements, or simply how much the stock price fluctuated over a one year period
- It's how much the stock price fluctuated of a day to day (over a one year period)

### Implied Volatility
- Implied volatility isn't based on historical prices but on the market price of the option
- Implied volatility is what the market place is implying the volatility of the stock will be in the future based on price changes
- The stock listed at its value is historically based on a consensus of more interest to total option traders
- Implied volatility and option prices change based on supply and demand activity in the marketplace; usually all implied volatility will increase activity when option will increase volatility; so when we look for the price of an option seller, its often goes for the other option owner
