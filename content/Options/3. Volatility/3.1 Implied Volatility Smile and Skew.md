
## What is an Implied Volatility Smile?

- In theory, the Black-Scholes model assumes that volatility is constant across all strike prices
- Instead, implied volatility in real markets is also not as low
- Real implied volatility changes differently, so it reflects real portfolio hedging and demand
- When implied volatility is plotted across different strikes, it typically forms either a "Smile" or a "Skew" — these slopes show how volatility varies depending on where the option sits relative to the underlying price

## A Volatility Smile is a U-shaped curve where implied volatility:
- Is higher at the far out-of-the-money and deep out-of-the-money options
- Is lower near the at-the-money options and deep out-of-the-money options
- The shape suggests that the market is feeling in more risk at the extremes

## A Volatility Skew is a U-shaped curve where:
- Implied volatility is a consistent curve where implied volatility is higher at out-of-the-money puts
- Traders are more willing to pay for downside protection (puts)
- Options are more willing to pay for insurance policies because hedgers are more fearful, and there is more demand for puts
- Implied volatility Puts (hedges) have higher implied volatility higher

## These Reflects are not random — they reflect real behaviour
- The higher implied volatility in fact for example shows that the market believes in downside
- The market is more concerned about downside risk than while offensively getting ahead
- The market is often considered a measure of fear or buying ahead
